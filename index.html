The thesis

A name for weddings in general — for the whole trade, not one corner of it.

Vow7 suits a wedding platform, a registry, a planning service, a stationer, a venue group, or a planner's signature line, because every one of those businesses sells the same thing at its core: two people standing up and meaning it.

The seven is not a compromise. Seven is the ritual number of weddings across traditions — seven vows, seven blessings, seven steps — and seven has always been the luck number. A digit that carries meaning reads differently from a digit that fills a gap. Vow7 does not spell like a name that settled. It reads like a name that chose its number.

And it reads just as naturally for the trade's least-contested corner: vow renewals. Couples renewing at ten, twenty-five, or fifty years are not buying a smaller first wedding. They are buying a second beginning, deliberately, with witnesses — and no incumbent brand owns that category yet. A name that works for a first wedding and a renewal, for a platform and a planner, is a name with more than one buyer. That is the point.

Four characters. Short enough to say on the phone without spelling it. Short enough to print on an invitation, a storefront, or a card without shrinking the type. Short enough to remember from a single hearing. The best wedding brands are the ones guests remember on the way home. This one gives them almost nothing to forget.
